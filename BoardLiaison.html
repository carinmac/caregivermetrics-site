The Sisters built hospitals for people

That work is not finished. The organizations CHSO stewards exist because someone decided that compassion was not enough on its own. It had to be built into structure, governance, and the way institutions make decisions. That is the mission I understand. This is how I came to understand it.

The inheritance

My uncle spent thirty years inside Catholic institutions building systems for the students nobody had a program for yet.

He worked at the Metropolitan Toronto Catholic School Board, at Loretto College School and Loretto Abbey, at the Hospital for Sick Children. In 1993 he opened the first Alternative School in the board's history. He authored the provincial classroom management guidance that Ontario teachers used for years. He trained educators in non-violent crisis intervention. He did all of this inside Catholic institutions, carrying forward what those institutions were founded to do.

His volunteer work with Fontbonne Ministries connected him to the Sisters of St. Joseph's community work in Toronto. My other uncle was a priest. These are not abstract affiliations. This is the culture I grew up adjacent to, watching compassion become structure, watching people choose the harder, slower work of building something that would hold.
